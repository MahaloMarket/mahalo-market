How to Tell Real Kona Coffee From a Blend

The label says "Kona." Here is what it really means, and how to choose a bag you will enjoy.

By the Mahalo Market team · 5 min read

Kona coffee is grown on the slopes of Hualālai and Mauna Loa, in the Kona district of the Big Island of Hawaii. Because it is rare and in demand, you will also find bags that say "Kona" in large letters while containing only a small share of Kona beans. Knowing how to read the label is the easiest way to get what you paid for.

What "100% Kona" means

A bag labeled 100% Kona coffee contains only coffee grown in the Kona district. It is the purest option, and it is usually the most expensive. Some producers also list farm names, harvest dates or roast levels on the bag, which are helpful signs that the coffee was traceable.

What a Kona blend is

A Kona blend mixes Kona beans with coffee from somewhere else. Hawaii's labeling rules require blends to state how much Kona coffee they contain, so look for the percentage printed on the front or back. A 10% Kona blend can still be a pleasant everyday coffee, but it is not the same experience as a 100% bag.

Check the label firstLook for "100% Kona coffee" or a clear Kona percentage. If the bag says only "Kona style" or "Hawaiian blend" without a number, ask yourself what is inside before you buy.

Whole bean or ground?

Whole bean coffee keeps its aroma longer. Grind it right before brewing and the difference is easy to taste. Ground coffee is convenient, but it loses freshness faster. If you choose whole bean, buy a bag that lists a roast date, and store it in an airtight container away from heat and sunlight.

Roast levels for Kona

  • Light to medium: brighter, with fruit and floral notes. Good for pour-over and drip.
  • Medium: balanced and smooth. A friendly choice if you are new to Kona.
  • Medium-dark: richer and more chocolatey. Works well as an espresso or a milk coffee.

Gift it, or brew it at home

A bag of 100% Kona is one of the most respected gifts for coffee lovers. Pair it with a simple brewer and a note about the farm, and the gift feels personal.
