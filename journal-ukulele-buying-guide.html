How to Choose Your First Ukulele

Sizes, starter kits and the few accessories that matter, explained without the jargon.

By the Mahalo Market team · 6 min read

Almost everyone who picks up a ukulele for the first time asks the same question: which one should I buy? The good news is that a beginner ukulele does not need to be expensive. The right choice depends mostly on size, the kind of sound you like, and whether the instrument comes with what you need to start playing.

Start with the size

Ukuleles come in four common sizes. The differences are mostly about how much room your fingers have and how much volume and sustain the instrument has.

  • Soprano (about 21 inches): the classic small ukulele with a bright, plinky sound. Compact and light, but the frets are close together.
  • Concert (about 23 inches): the most popular choice for beginners. A little more space between frets and a fuller tone, while still easy to hold.
  • Tenor (about 26 inches): deeper sound and more room for bigger hands, often used by players who want a warmer voice.
  • Baritone (about 30 inches): tuned lower, like the top four strings of a guitar. Useful for some players, but most beginners do not need it.

If you are unsure, a concert ukulele is a safe first pick. It is the size most beginner songbooks and video lessons are written for.

Beginner kit or single instrument?

Many listings for beginners are "kits" or "bundles." A good kit usually includes a gig bag, a clip-on or digital tuner, a strap, spare strings and a pick. Those extras add up quickly if you buy them one by one, so a kit can be the better value.

Look for a kit that lists the parts clearly. Check that the gig bag has padding and that the tuner is included. Avoid kits where the photo shows accessories that the description does not mention.

TipTune the ukulele before every practice session. Most beginners who struggle with a new instrument are playing out of tune, not playing badly.

Wood and build: what matters for a beginner

Solid-top ukuleles sound better, but they cost more and can be affected by humidity. Laminate (plywood) bodies are sturdier and cheaper, and many beginner ukuleles use them. Mahogany is a common, warm-sounding wood for entry-level instruments. Koa, the traditional Hawaiian wood, is beautiful and sounds rich, and it is worth considering once you know you will keep playing.

Whatever you choose, make sure the strings sit comfortably above the fretboard. If they buzz or feel hard to press, a setup at a music shop can help.

Learn the first chords

Most songs you will want to play use only a few chords. Start with C, F, G and A minor. Practice switching between them slowly until the change feels smooth. Then try a Hawaiian classic. Many of the island's best-known songs are built on simple progressions, and they make a good first goal. If you want to go further, slack key guitar offers a different sound that many ukulele players enjoy exploring next.

Our recommendation

For most people, buy a concert ukulele in a complete beginner kit. Pick a size you are comfortable holding, make sure the kit includes a tuner and a bag, and practice for ten minutes a day. You will notice progress faster than you expect.
