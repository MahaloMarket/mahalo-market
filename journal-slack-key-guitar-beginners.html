What Is Slack Key Guitar, and Where Should You Start?

The easygoing sound of Hawaiian slack key, what makes it different, and the albums to listen to first.

By the Mahalo Market team · 6 min read

If you have heard a Hawaiian guitar and thought it sounded open, warm and a little bit like the ocean, you were probably hearing slack key. It is one of the most recognizable sounds of the islands, and it is easier to start listening to than most people expect.

What "slack key" means

In Hawaiian, slack key guitar is called ki hoʻalu. The name comes from the practice of "slacking" the strings, loosening some of them to a different tuning before playing. Players use many open tunings, and each one lets the guitar ring with a full chord when a string is strummed or plucked without fretting. The result is a rich, resonant sound that often sounds like two guitars playing at once.

The tunings are often passed down within families, and many players have their own versions. That is part of what makes the style personal: two players can start from the same song and sound quite different.

How it is played

Slack key players often use their thumb to play a steady bass line while the fingers pick a melody on the higher strings. Songs are usually simple in structure, but the rhythm and the way the notes ring together give the music its character. Many players also sing, so the guitar and voice share the melody.

Listen for thisNotice how the bass notes keep moving underneath the melody. That steady pulse is one of the easiest things to recognize once you know to listen for it.

Where a beginner should start

Begin with a compilation rather than a single artist. A good collection introduces several styles and players in one place. Two popular starting points are the Hawaiian Slack Key Guitar Masters collections, which bring together many well-known players, and an instrumental collection if you want to focus on the guitar alone.

After you have a few favorites, look for albums by individual artists. That is when you will start to hear the differences in tuning and phrasing.

Should you learn to play it?

You do not need to start on a slack key guitar. Many people begin on a ukulele, which has a similar bright tone and is easier to hold. Once you know a few chords, slack key ideas, such as open tunings and simple bass patterns, become easier to understand. Some players later move to a steel-string or classical guitar for the full sound.

If you prefer to listen first, that is a great way to learn too. Put an album on while you cook or read, and let the sound become familiar.

A simple listening plan

  1. Week 1: listen to one instrumental compilation and note the songs you return to.
  2. Week 2: pick one artist from that compilation and listen to a full album.
  3. Week 3: try a live recording to hear the room, the audience and the rhythm of a real performance.
